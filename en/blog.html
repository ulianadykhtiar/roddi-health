A blog about the health of rodents

Nutrition, symptoms, care and emergencies, short and clear. Choose your pet or search by keyword.

Respiratory infections in rats: signs to watch for

Sneezing, wheezing or “clicking” when breathing, nasal discharge and reddish crusts around the eyes are the most common signals. Left untreated, an infection can be dangerous, so these symptoms call for a vet visit.

PetMD

Mycoplasmosis: why a rat struggles to breathe

Many rats carry mycoplasma and show no signs for years, with flare-ups triggered by stress. A dirty cage that smells of ammonia irritates the airways, so regular cleaning is part of prevention.

Walkerville Vet

What to feed a rat and how to watch its weight

A complete pelleted rat food is the basis of the diet, with vegetables, a little fruit and protein treats as extras. Rats gain weight easily, so it’s worth controlling portions.

Blue Cross

How to make a rat’s life more interesting

Rats are smart and curious, and they need new experiences. Boxes, tunnels, ropes and scattered food to forage for all help prevent boredom.

Blue Cross

Wet tail in hamsters: why it’s urgent

A wet, soiled tail, lethargy and refusing food can mean a severe intestinal infection. The condition worsens quickly, so a hamster needs to see a vet within the first day.

Ask A Vet

The 25 most common questions about hamsters

Vets answer what owners ask most: what cage is needed, what to feed, whether two hamsters can live together and how to tell if one is ill.

VetVerified

Why guinea pigs need vitamin C

Like humans, guinea pigs can’t make their own vitamin C, so they need it from food every day. Bell peppers and leafy greens are good sources. Vitamin C added to drinking water is unreliable.

SPCA New Zealand

Scurvy in guinea pigs: how to spot it in time

Signs of vitamin C deficiency can appear after just 2–3 weeks of poor diet: stiffness, joint pain, bleeding gums and a poor coat. It’s treatable if caught early.

Unusual Pet Vets

Basic guinea pig care

Unlimited hay, fresh vegetables, guinea pig pellets and clean water are the basis of the diet. They also need a roomy cage and company, since they’re social animals.

RSPCA Australia

Chinchilla dust baths: how and how often

Chinchillas aren’t bathed in water because their dense fur dries poorly. Instead, offer special bathing dust and remove the bath after about half an hour to avoid irritating the eyes.

PetMD

Chinchilla health: teeth, coat and digestion

A chinchilla’s teeth grow throughout life, so it needs unlimited hay and safe things to chew. Drooling or refusing food are reasons to have a vet check the teeth.

PDSA

Degus and sugar: how to prevent diabetes

Degus process sugar poorly and are prone to diabetes. It’s best to avoid fruit, dried fruit and sweet vegetables. Hay and degu food should form the basis of the diet. Diabetes is easier to prevent than to treat.

Exotic Direct

Degus at home: what owners need to know

Degus are active by day and live in groups. They need space, a wheel, things to chew and dust baths. Excessive thirst or cloudy eyes are reasons to see a vet.

PetMD

Mouse health: a one-minute daily check

Mice often hide pain, so it’s best to catch changes early: check the eyes, nose, coat, breathing and droppings. Sneezing or “chattering” when breathing are reasons to see a vet.

PDSA

Routine care for pet mice

An owner’s guide from the MSD Vet Manual: how to set up housing, how often to clean, what to feed and how to notice the first signs of illness.

MSD Vet Manual

GI stasis in rabbits: when to raise the alarm

A rabbit that isn’t eating and hasn’t passed droppings for several hours needs an urgent vet visit. The best prevention is a diet based on hay, with only small amounts of pellets and fruit.

VCA Animal Hospitals

Rabbit not eating: what to do

A rabbit refusing food is no small matter: digestion can stop within hours. We explain the possible causes and why you shouldn’t wait until morning.

Walkerville Vet
