A blog about the health of rodents

Nutrition, symptoms, care and emergencies, short and clear. Choose your pet or search by keyword.

Why is my rat sneezing, and when to see a vet

Even an occasional sneeze or reddish crusts around the eyes and nose can be the first signs of a respiratory infection. According to RSPCA Australia, it is the most common health problem in rats, and untreated it can turn into pneumonia. So show a sneezing rat to a vet rather than waiting.

What to feed a rat: what’s allowed and what isn’t

The basis of a pet rat’s diet is a pelleted food made specifically for rats. Vegetables, a little fruit, boiled egg, grains and seeds are given within the daily ration, not on top of it. Grapes, raisins, onion and chocolate are not suitable.

What to feed a hamster: what’s allowed and what isn’t

Most of a hamster’s diet should be a pelleted hamster food. Fresh food is given in moderation, because too much of it can cause diarrhoea, which is dangerous for a hamster: it loses fluid quickly.

Wet tail in hamsters: symptoms and what to do

“Wet tail” is diarrhoea that soaks the fur under the tail. A hamster loses fluid quickly, so it is often an emergency: PetMD advises that a vet who treats exotic animals should see the hamster, ideally within 24 hours.

Guinea pig not eating: what to do

If your guinea pig has stopped eating or is producing fewer droppings, call a vet straight away. According to PetMD, untreated gut stasis can become fatal within 24–48 hours.

What to feed a guinea pig and where to get vitamin C

Hay and grass make up most of a guinea pig’s diet and must always be available: PetMD puts their share at about 80%, PDSA at 85–90%. Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C themselves, so they get it from food every day, above all from fresh vegetables.

How to check a pet mouse’s health: daily check and warning signs

Mice hide illness, so by the time symptoms are obvious a mouse is often already seriously ill. A short check every morning and evening helps you spot changes early: dull eyes, a ruffled coat, unusual breathing, weight loss or a mouse that sits apart from the others.

Pet mice: housing, cleaning, food and company

Mice are social, nocturnal animals. They need same-sex companions, a roomy, well-ventilated home without draughts, and places to hide and climb. The core of the diet is a pelleted mouse food, with fresh greens within the daily ration and clean water every day.

Dust baths for chinchillas: why, how often and for how long

Chinchillas clean themselves in dust, which takes excess oil and moisture out of their dense fur. Bathing them in water is a bad idea: the fur takes a long time to dry and the skin can become inflamed. A bath of special dust is offered for a limited time and then removed so the dust doesn’t irritate the eyes.

Chinchilla health: teeth, fur, digestion and warning signs

A chinchilla’s teeth grow throughout life, so the core of its diet is unlimited hay, not sugary mixes. Saliva on the chin, a poorer appetite, weight loss or fewer droppings can mean mouth pain or digestive trouble. Chinchillas hide illness, so these signs are a reason to see a vet straight away.

Scurvy in guinea pigs: signs of vitamin C deficiency and prevention

Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C themselves, so they need it from food every day. According to PetMD, signs of deficiency can appear after just 2–3 weeks of a poor diet: the coat, appetite and weight get worse, wounds heal slowly and moving becomes painful. A vet finds the cause and prescribes treatment.

Basic guinea pig care: hay, food, housing and company

The core of a guinea pig’s diet is unlimited hay and grass. On top of that come a few guinea pig pellets, fresh vegetables every day and clean water. Guinea pigs are social and live in pairs or groups. They need a roomy home with a solid floor, hides and space to move.

Degus at home: housing, company, diet and health

Degus are active by day and live in groups, so they are not kept alone. They need a tall metal cage with several levels and a wheel, deep bedding for digging and dust baths a few times a week. The core of the diet is hay, with a few sugar-free pellets, because degus are prone to diabetes.

How to make a rat’s life more interesting: company, play and foraging

Rats are clever, curious and social, so they need at least one companion and new experiences every day. A tall cage with several levels, hides, ropes, digging boxes and food they have to search for help prevent boredom, and time outside the cage should always be supervised.

A hamster for beginners: answers to common questions

Syrian hamsters live alone, while dwarf hamsters can sometimes live in pairs if they grew up together. Hamsters need a roomy cage with deep bedding for digging, a solid wheel that keeps their back straight, pelleted food and quiet during the day, as they are active at night.
