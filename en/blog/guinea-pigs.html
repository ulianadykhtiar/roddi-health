Guinea pigs: health, food and care

Articles about guinea pigs are collected here: urgent, nutrition, symptoms, care. Every article lists its sources and the date the facts were checked. They are reference material and do not replace a vet.

Urgent

Guinea pig not eating: what to do

If your guinea pig has stopped eating or is producing fewer droppings, call a vet straight away. According to PetMD, untreated gut stasis can become fatal within 24–48 hours.

Nutrition

What to feed a guinea pig and where to get vitamin C

Hay and grass make up most of a guinea pig’s diet and must always be available: PetMD puts their share at about 80%, PDSA at 85–90%. Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C themselves, so they get it from food every day, above all from fresh vegetables.

Symptoms

Scurvy in guinea pigs: signs of vitamin C deficiency and prevention

Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C themselves, so they need it from food every day. According to PetMD, signs of deficiency can appear after just 2–3 weeks of a poor diet: the coat, appetite and weight get worse, wounds heal slowly and moving becomes painful. A vet finds the cause and prescribes treatment.

Care

Basic guinea pig care: hay, food, housing and company

The core of a guinea pig’s diet is unlimited hay and grass. On top of that come a few guinea pig pellets, fresh vegetables every day and clean water. Guinea pigs are social and live in pairs or groups. They need a roomy home with a solid floor, hides and space to move.
