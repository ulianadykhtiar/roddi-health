Dust baths for chinchillas: why, how often and for how long

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

Chinchillas clean themselves in dust, which takes excess oil and moisture out of their dense fur. Bathing them in water is a bad idea: the fur takes a long time to dry and the skin can become inflamed. A bath of special dust is offered for a limited time and then removed so the dust doesn’t irritate the eyes.

Why chinchillas need dust

A chinchilla’s fur is extremely dense: several dozen hairs grow from a single follicle (PetMD says up to 60, the RSPCA says 60–90). It is an adaptation to cold high mountains. By rolling in dust, a chinchilla removes excess skin oil, dirt and moisture from its coat. PDSA warns that without these baths the fur can become matted. A bath is also enrichment.

Why not water

Dense fur dries slowly and holds moisture. According to PetMD, this can lead to matting and inflamed skin. That is why chinchillas are not bathed or wetted.

Which dust to choose

  • Use dust or sand made specifically for chinchilla baths. According to PetMD, it is a fine, cleaned powder made from volcanic ash, pumice, kaolin or silver sand.
  • Ordinary sand and similar products not meant for chinchillas are too abrasive and can irritate the skin, eyes and airways.
  • Extra-fine dust can irritate the nose and eyes, especially in chinchillas under a year old.
  • Replace the dust when it gets clumpy.

The bath bowl

Use an open-top bowl or tray the chinchilla can roll around in freely, such as a shallow metal bowl. A closed box traps moisture. Sources differ on depth: PetMD suggests about 2.5 cm (1 inch), PDSA 4–6 cm.

How often and how long

All sources agree on one thing: the bath does not stay in the cage all the time. Agree the frequency with your vet and watch the condition of the coat and skin. PetMD warns that going more than a week without a bath can lead to excess oil and skin irritation.

Why the bath is removed

According to PetMD, constant access to dust can cause conjunctivitis and dry skin. Chinchillas may also use the bath as a toilet, so the RSPCA advises removing or sieving the dust after use.

Room conditions

The RSPCA advises keeping chinchillas indoors in a dry, well-ventilated, draught-free room, ideally at 10–18 °C: heat, humidity and draughts can make them ill. PetMD adds that humidity should stay below 40–50% and the cage should be out of direct sunlight.

When to see a vet

Take your chinchilla to a vet if you notice bald patches, discharge from the eyes or nose, or wet fur around the eyes or mouth. See our chinchilla health article for other warning signs.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • how often and how long the bath lasts
  • which dust you use
  • the condition of the eyes and coat
  • room temperature and humidity
