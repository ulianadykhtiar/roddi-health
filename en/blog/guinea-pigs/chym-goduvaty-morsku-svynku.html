What to feed a guinea pig and where to get vitamin C

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

Hay and grass make up most of a guinea pig’s diet and must always be available: PetMD puts their share at about 80%, PDSA at 85–90%. Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C themselves, so they get it from food every day, above all from fresh vegetables.

Hay and grass

Hay must always be available. RSPCA Australia suggests a daily pile of hay twice the size of the guinea pig. Almost any grass hay is suitable. Sources disagree about alfalfa: PetMD advises avoiding it because regular eating can cause harm, while RSPCA Australia allows it for young, pregnant, nursing and ill guinea pigs, and only occasionally for others. Fresh grass is good, but lawnmower clippings must never be fed.

Pellets

Pellets are given in limited amounts: PDSA suggests about a tablespoon per adult guinea pig a day. Choose grass-based pellets fortified with vitamin C and check they are fresh, because the vitamin breaks down over time. Sources define the limit differently: PDSA says three months after purchase, PetMD’s care sheet three months after production, the RSPCA by the best-before date.

Vegetables and fruit

Fresh vegetables are given every day, but sources give different amounts: PDSA suggests about a cup per guinea pig, PetMD a quarter to half a cup. Fruit is where they disagree most: SPCA New Zealand recommends plenty of fresh fruit and vegetables daily, PetMD fruit once or twice a week, PDSA fruit as a treat once every week or two. Introduce new food gradually: PDSA suggests changing the diet over two to four weeks.

Where to get vitamin C

Guinea pigs can’t make vitamin C, so they need it every day. SPCA New Zealand considers pellets and vitamin C in drinking water unreliable sources because it breaks down quickly, and recommends fresh vegetables. The RSPCA and PDSA treat fortified pellets as a source. PDSA adds that a supplement may help if you’re unsure your guinea pig gets enough, but it shouldn’t be added to water. See our scurvy article for the signs of deficiency.

Greens high in calcium

SPCA New Zealand recommends leafy greens such as kale, parsley and spinach for vitamin C. PetMD’s care sheet warns that these are high in calcium, and guinea pigs are prone to bladder stones. The RSPCA allows parsley often and kale and spinach as treats. Ask your vet how much of these greens suits your guinea pig.

What not to give

FoodWho advises avoiding it
Iceberg lettuce, potato, garlic, avocadoRSPCA, PDSA
Onion, mushrooms, chivesPDSA, PetMD
RhubarbPDSA
Chocolate, daffodils, buttercups, liliesPetMD
Bread, biscuits, cakes, grains, seeds, nutsPetMD, RSPCA Australia
Citrus, coconutRSPCA
Lawnmower clippingsRSPCA, PDSA, RSPCA Australia

Sources disagree about peas, beans and corn: PetMD counts them as starchy foods to avoid, while RSPCA Australia lists them among vegetables that can be fed.

Water

Fresh water must always be available. The RSPCA advises checking it twice a day and making sure it doesn’t freeze in winter. PetMD advises changing bowl water daily.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • which pellets and how long the bag has been open
  • which vegetables and how much you give daily
  • whether you give vitamin C separately
  • weight over time
