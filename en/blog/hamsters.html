Hamsters: health, food and care

Articles about hamsters are collected here: nutrition, urgent, care. Every article lists its sources and the date the facts were checked. They are reference material and do not replace a vet.

Nutrition

What to feed a hamster: what’s allowed and what isn’t

Most of a hamster’s diet should be a pelleted hamster food. Fresh food is given in moderation, because too much of it can cause diarrhoea, which is dangerous for a hamster: it loses fluid quickly.

Urgent

Wet tail in hamsters: symptoms and what to do

“Wet tail” is diarrhoea that soaks the fur under the tail. A hamster loses fluid quickly, so it is often an emergency: PetMD advises that a vet who treats exotic animals should see the hamster, ideally within 24 hours.

Care

A hamster for beginners: answers to common questions

Syrian hamsters live alone, while dwarf hamsters can sometimes live in pairs if they grew up together. Hamsters need a roomy cage with deep bedding for digging, a solid wheel that keeps their back straight, pelleted food and quiet during the day, as they are active at night.
