Why is my rat sneezing, and when to see a vet

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

Even an occasional sneeze or reddish crusts around the eyes and nose can be the first signs of a respiratory infection. According to RSPCA Australia, it is the most common health problem in rats, and untreated it can turn into pneumonia. So show a sneezing rat to a vet rather than waiting.

If your animal’s condition gets worse, see a vet straight away. Don’t put off the visit and don’t treat your pet yourself based on this article.

See a vet urgently if:

  • wheezing, crackling or clicking when breathing
  • breathing with the belly or with an open mouth
  • hunched posture, weight loss
  • head tilt, loss of balance

Early signs

At first the symptoms are easy to miss: the rat sneezes now and then, and small reddish crusts appear around the eyes or nose. This is dried red pigment from tears, called porphyrin. Within a few days you may also see:

  • frequent sneezing and squinting
  • rusty tears or nasal discharge
  • poorer appetite and lethargy
  • a ruffled coat
  • behaviour changes: the rat avoids contact, is irritable or bites

Red staining around the eyes and nose can also come from stress. The RSPCA says a small amount that doesn’t recur is not a worry, while a lot of it can point to a serious problem.

Why it happens

According to PetMD, most rats naturally carry the bacterium Mycoplasma pulmonis, one of the most common causes of these infections. It passes from rat to rat, and a rat can carry it without any signs. Viruses can also cause disease, and other bacteria join in when immunity is weakened. The New South Wales government schools guide warns that the infection is highly contagious.

The risk rises with age, other illnesses, obesity, stress and overcrowding, ammonia in a dirty cage, and smoke, vaping, scented soap, household chemicals and candles. Pine and cedar bedding irritate the airways, and PDSA warns against any dusty bedding. PetMD considers paper bedding the safer option.

Why not to wait

PetMD advises that any rat with signs of a respiratory infection should see a vet straight away, and PDSA warns that these infections can get worse very quickly. RSPCA Australia says that once signs are visible, the changes in the lungs are permanent and treatment may be needed for life. PetMD knows of no home remedies that cure the infection, so only give medicines a vet has prescribed.

While you wait for the appointment

  • Clean the cage and remove strong smells nearby.
  • Note down when the symptoms started and what has changed.
  • You can take a short video of the breathing to show the vet.
  • Don’t give medicines or antibiotics without a prescription.

How to lower the risk

  • Keep new rats apart and watch them: PetMD advises at least 2 weeks, the New South Wales schools guide 4 weeks.
  • Clean the cage regularly so ammonia doesn’t build up.
  • Keep the cage somewhere quiet and clean, well ventilated but free of draughts.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • when the sneezing started and how many times a day
  • a video of the breathing
  • current weight and any change in appetite
  • whether new rats arrived
  • which bedding you use and any strong smells
