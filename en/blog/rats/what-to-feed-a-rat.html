What to feed a rat: what’s allowed and what isn’t

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

The basis of a pet rat’s diet is a pelleted food made specifically for rats. Vegetables, a little fruit, boiled egg, grains and seeds are given within the daily ration, not on top of it. Grapes, raisins, onion and chocolate are not suitable.

What the diet is based on

Choose pellets made specifically for rats. The RSPCA warns that rabbit, guinea pig or hamster food won’t give rats the nutrients they need. Rats are omnivores, so the diet can be topped up with small amounts of vegetables, fruit, boiled egg, grains and seeds.

These extras come out of the daily portion rather than being added to it. PetMD advises that all extras and treats together make up no more than 10% of the diet: rats gain weight easily. Take the amount of the main food from the instructions on the pack and weigh your rat to keep an eye on its weight.

How to feed

  • Twice a day, morning and evening. The RSPCA explains that rats eat mostly at dawn and dusk.
  • Scatter some food, mix it into the bedding or hide it in cardboard tubes so the rats forage for it.
  • The RSPCA recommends ceramic bowls: metal ones give off ultrasound that bothers rats.
  • Remove what hasn’t been eaten. PetMD advises throwing away leftover vegetables and fruit after 10 hours at most.
  • Sweet and fatty foods only as an occasional reward during training. PetMD advises avoiding such treats altogether.

Water

Fresh, clean water must always be available. The RSPCA recommends drinking bottles rather than bowls, several of them, checked twice a day. RSPCA Australia and PetMD advise changing the water daily.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • exactly what you feed and how much a day
  • your rat’s weight over time
  • which treats you gave recently
