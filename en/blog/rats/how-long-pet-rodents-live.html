How long pet rodents live: rats, hamsters, guinea pigs, degus

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

Mice and hamsters live the shortest, about 1–3 years, rats 2–3 years, guinea pigs 4–8, degus 5–9 years, and chinchillas, according to the RSPCA, 10–20 years. Sources give different figures, so treat these as guides, not exact limits.

These are approximate averages with proper care. Sources differ noticeably, especially for mice and guinea pigs, and individual animals live shorter or longer.

What matters most

  • A diet that suits the species, without too much sugar and fat.
  • A clean home without strong smells or draughts.
  • Companions for social species, exercise and calm handling.
  • Regular check-ups: PetMD advises a vet visit for hamsters once a year, and the RSPCA at least once a year for guinea pigs.
  • Seeing a vet at the first changes: the RSPCA warns that mice and hamsters lose condition quickly when ill.

Why keeping records matters

These animals often hide illness, so changes in weight or appetite may be the only early sign. Regular notes help you spot a trend and show it to the vet.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • the animal’s age
  • weight over time
  • past illnesses and treatment
