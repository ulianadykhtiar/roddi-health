What to feed a degu: hay, pellets and go easy on sugar

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

The basis of a degu’s diet is unlimited good-quality hay. Pellets are given in small amounts and without molasses, because degus are prone to diabetes. Fruit and starchy vegetables are best limited or left out.

What the diet is based on

Most of the diet is hay, available at all times, such as timothy or meadow hay. Hay wears down the continuously growing teeth and supports the gut. Exotic Direct advises against brown or green hay, because green hay can cause bloating, and says a pink or white coating means mould.

Degu pellets are given in limited amounts: PDSA suggests one to two tablespoons, about 10 g a day, and PetMD one to two tablespoons depending on size and activity. If degu pellets aren’t available, guinea pig or chinchilla pellets without molasses will do. The RSPCA considers rabbit, hamster and gerbil food, and mixes with dried fruit, unsuitable.

Vegetables and fruit

PDSA suggests a small portion of leafy greens and vegetables once or twice a week, no more. The RSPCA and PetMD advise against fruit because of its sugar, while PDSA allows an occasional bit of apple. Introduce new foods gradually.

Sources disagree, so if in doubt follow the stricter option and talk to your vet.

Diabetes

Degus are prone to type 2 diabetes, especially on a diet high in sugar and carbohydrates. PDSA and Exotic Direct list these signs: drinking and urinating more, cloudy eyes (cataracts), and, according to PDSA, weight gain. If you notice them, see a vet.

Water and vitamin C

Change the water daily, as PDSA and Exotic Direct advise. Exotic Direct advises against adding vitamin C to the water, because you can’t control how much the degu drinks and the vitamin breaks down quickly. If needed, it can come from food such as broccoli or red pepper.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • the ingredients of the food
  • what you give besides hay and how often
  • whether it drinks more than usual or its eyes look cloudy
  • weight over time
