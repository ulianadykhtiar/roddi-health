Pet mice: housing, cleaning, food and company

Roddi Health editorial team · updated 16 November 2026 · overview of open sources

In short

Mice are social, nocturnal animals. They need same-sex companions, a roomy, well-ventilated home without draughts, and places to hide and climb. The core of the diet is a pelleted mouse food, with fresh greens within the daily ration and clean water every day.

Company

Mice are social and should live with other mice. Keep stable single-sex groups. Males are often aggressive towards unfamiliar males, so a male group is best made of brothers who grew up together. Females usually accept newcomers more easily. A male lives with females only if he is neutered. The RSPCA advises keeping a mouse alone only if a vet says so, and not housing other rodents with mice.

Housing

  • Sources give no exact floor area for pet mice. They advise as much space as possible, and PDSA considers most pet-shop cages too small.
  • The home needs good airflow without draughts. Ammonia builds up in glass tanks and solid plastic boxes.
  • Bar spacing of no more than 5 mm (RSPCA Australia) or 6 mm (PDSA).
  • A solid floor: wire mesh hurts their feet.
  • Several levels, ladders and ropes: mice are good climbers.
  • Several hides with two exits, and a dark spot for a nest.

Where to put it

PDSA and the New South Wales schools guide give 18–22 °C as a comfortable range, without sudden swings. Keep the cage out of direct sun and draughts, and out of bathrooms, garages and conservatories. Mice need quiet, away from the TV and loud music, as they are sensitive to noise and light. RSPCA Australia suggests 8–12 hours of dark, quiet time a day.

Sources disagree about wood shavings: RSPCA Victoria and the New South Wales schools guide allow them, while the RSPCA, PDSA and RSPCA Australia advise against them. Paper bedding is the safer choice.

Cleaning

  • Remove droppings and wet bedding every day.
  • PDSA recommends a full clean every week; the RSPCA says every one to two weeks.
  • Mice scent-mark their territory. Put back a little dry old bedding so the home still smells familiar.
  • Clean in the evening, when mice wake up.
  • Check the water bottle morning and evening: mice can block the spout with bedding.

What to feed

  • The core of the diet is mouse pellets or blocks. Each pellet contains everything needed, so a mouse cannot pick out only the tasty bits.
  • RSPCA Australia and RSPCA Victoria advise against seed mixes: mice pick their favourite parts, which leads to imbalance and obesity. PDSA allows mixes but suggests switching to pellets if a mouse leaves part of the food.
  • Fresh dark greens and non-starchy vegetables in small portions, as part of the daily ration rather than on top of it.
  • Treats should be no more than 10% of calories.
  • Don’t give chocolate, grapes or raisins, avocado, garlic, onion, coffee, tea, alcohol, sweets, fatty or dairy foods, or sticky food.
  • Scatter some food so the mice can forage for it.
  • Fresh water every day. RSPCA Australia recommends a drinking bottle, ideally two in case one fails.
  • Introduce new food gradually.

Gnawing and exercise

Mice’s incisors grow throughout life, so they need things to gnaw made of untreated wood or cardboard. A wheel should be solid, with no gaps or rungs, and big enough for the mouse to run with a straight back. The RSPCA reminds owners that a wheel shouldn’t be the only enrichment: add tunnels, boxes and ropes.

What to note down before you call the vet

  • how many mice live together and their sex
  • cage size and type
  • which bedding and food you use
  • how often you clean
  • room temperature
